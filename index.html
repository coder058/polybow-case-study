Polymarket offered short-duration BTC markets where a tiny price gap could look like an edge. But a $0.99 share leaves only about one cent before fees, spread, latency and a disappearing order book. The question was simple: could software turn that theoretical gap into a repeatable, fillable trade?

The answer changed as the bot met real execution. Recalculating the full ledger… This is market-resolution accounting, not settled wallet cash or proof of a durable edge.

02 / BUILD + ITERATION LOG

The bot changed whenever the evidence disagreed.

The work moved from a polling prototype to event-driven execution, from expensive favorites to asymmetric cheap fills, and finally to controlled variants. AI was used as an engineering multiplier for decomposition, implementation, debugging, refactoring and analysis; it was not the market oracle, a trading signal or a substitute for the ledger.

BUILD 01 / THE LOOP

MAKE THE TRADE POSSIBLE

I built market discovery, Chainlink/RTDS opening-price capture, probability checks, order-book reads, CLOB execution, resolution handling, a monitor and a post-trade ledger. The first version relied too heavily on polling and stale snapshots: a research loop had to become an execution system.

FIRST LIVE RUN / 108 FIRES

ACCURACY WAS NOT ENOUGH

The first live version targeted high-confidence BTC five-minute markets. The loop ran, but expensive favorite fills left almost no room after fees. The first lesson was execution economics: being right about direction was not the same as having positive expected value after getting filled.

STRATA → STRATB / 299 FIRES

CHEAPER, ASYMMETRIC FILLS

StratA and then StratB moved the search toward cheaper entries and a larger gap between the market price and the captured oracle move. The phase report records 299 StratB rank4 fires and 99 wins. It justified another test; it did not prove a durable edge.

EVENT-DRIVEN BUILD / WEBSOCKETS

REMOVE THE WAIT

Polling was replaced by an event-driven WebSocket path so a book update could trigger evaluation immediately. The system combined live book data, oracle timing and execution decisions. Historical latency notes are context only; the public evidence is the ledger and its reproducible analysis.

RUN-UP / 347 MARKETS

$221.40 RECONSTRUCTED

The anonymized ledger reconstructs a peak of $221.40 after market 347 from an $18 starting-balance assumption. A separate liquid-balance audit observed an approximate $210.22 peak. The run-up was concentrated in cheap BTC fills, and attribution to one bot is not complete.

OPEN TEST → UC / 0 FOR 8

THE EDGE DISAPPEARS

We opened the $0.01–$0.20 cheap-fill path and tested later gap and StratB variants. The reports include overlapping fire counts, not one failure total. UC recorded 0/8. The iterations pointed to fee drag, oracle timing, fillability and expanded exposure—not a new profitable system.

The public archive contains 1,590 trade rows across 1,137 markets. There is no defensible single “failed tries” total: local histories also contain non-fills, duplicated and backfilled records. The early phase counts above come from bot reports and are not identical to the final anonymized on-chain ledger. The on-chain ledger is authoritative for market activity.

03 / HOW AI ENTERED THE BUILD

AI was the multiplier.
Evidence was the brake.

01 / DECOMPOSE

Turn hypotheses into tasks.

Use AI to break a market question into capture, book, execution, ledger and monitor changes that can be inspected separately.

02 / ITERATE

Build, test, compare.

Use agents to implement and refactor variants, inspect traces and compare what changed. The strategy still had to face the actual fill and resolution records.

03 / VALIDATE

Reject plausible stories.

Use source data, regression tests, replay and on-chain checks to challenge the output. AI did not place trades, predict outcomes or turn a peak into a profitability claim.

04 / WHAT THE SYSTEM HAD TO SOLVE

The last cent was not free.

0.99

A share bought at $0.99 has roughly one cent of gross upside before fees and execution drag. Break-even is not just the ask: it is the ask plus taker fee plus slippage. It is not literally impossible to win; it is mathematically hostile to positive expected value.

CHAINLINK

The market did not resolve against whichever price was easiest to read. A broken snapshotter returned the wrong round, and the standard Polygon BTC/USD aggregator did not match Polymarket Data Streams. The fix was to capture the correct first report at or after open and align clocks with the price-to-beat.

THE BOOK

Best bid and ask were not enough. Quote size, disappearing liquidity, latency and slippage determined whether a theoretical edge could become a real fill. The monitor had to show the difference.

10 / CONTROL REVIEW

The edge disappeared after the patch.

The patch changed the conditions around the strategy. I kept testing the same idea and controlled variants to determine whether the edge was still alive and why execution no longer supported it.

Conclusion: the original edge was dead. The evidence pointed to fee drag, oracle timing, fillability and expanded exposure—not to a new profitable system.
