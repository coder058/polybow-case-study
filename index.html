01 / THE QUESTION

Could a one-cent edge survive contact with the market?

At $0.99, a winning share returns only about one cent before fees, spread and execution drag. One adverse resolution can erase roughly a hundred one-cent wins. The hard problem was not guessing whether BTC would move up or down; it was capturing the opening oracle value, reading a changing CLOB, finding a fillable price and doing all of it before the book disappeared.

MARCH–APR 16 / BUILD THE LOOP

THE SLOW VERSION

I built discovery, Chainlink/RTDS opening-price capture, probability checks, order-book reads, execution, resolution and a post-trade ledger. The first system relied too heavily on polling and stale snapshots. A roughly 27-second oracle update cycle was useful for research, not precision trading.

APR 16–23 / POLYBOW

108 FIRES

The first live version targeted high-confidence BTC five-minute markets. It proved the full loop could run, but expensive favorite fills left almost no room after fees. The lesson was execution economics: prediction accuracy alone was not the product.

APR 24–30 / STRATA → STRATB

299 FIRES / 99 WINS

StratA and then StratB moved the search toward asymmetric, cheaper fills and a larger gap between the market price and the captured oracle move. The phase report records 299 StratB rank4 fires and 99 wins. That result was enough to justify testing the mechanism, not enough to call it a proven edge.

EVENT-DRIVEN OPTIMIZATION

WS HOT EVAL

Polling was replaced by an event-driven WebSocket path: a book update could trigger evaluation immediately instead of waiting for the next loop. Historical notes describe a per-market gate, warm HTTP/2 connections and faster signing on a Dublin VPS. Those latency measurements are not reproduced by this public archive; its reproducible evidence is the ledger analysis below.

APR 16–MAY 2 / THE PEAK

347 MARKETS / $210.22

After the execution work, ultra-cheap BTC fills produced the concentrated run-up. The peak is visible in the reconstructed ledger and in the observed liquid-balance audit. The data does not prove that one bot caused every winning fill, so the page keeps that attribution limit visible.

MAY 2–17 / OPEN THE TEST

THE EDGE DISAPPEARS

On May 2 I removed the paper floor below $0.10, share-count caps, per-slug lock and paper-capital gate, opening the $0.01–$0.20 cheap-fill path for a real test. The later reports contain 113 gap5 fires, 32 gap10 fires, 112 StratB-variant fires, 55 later StratB fires and 8 UC fires; these overlap and are not one deduplicated failure total. UC was 0/8. I kept testing to determine why the edge was gone: fees, oracle timing, fillability and changed market conditions.

The public archive contains 1,590 trade rows across 1,137 markets. There is no defensible single “failed tries” total: local histories also contain non-fills, duplicated and backfilled records. The early phase counts above come from bot reports and are not identical to the final anonymized on-chain ledger. The on-chain ledger is authoritative for market activity.

02 / WHAT THE SYSTEM HAD TO SOLVE

The last cent was not free.

0.99

A share bought at $0.99 has roughly one cent of gross upside before fees and execution drag. Break-even is not just the ask: it is the ask plus taker fee plus slippage. It is not literally impossible to win; it is mathematically hostile to positive expected value.

CHAINLINK

The market did not resolve against whichever price was easiest to read. A broken snapshotter returned the wrong round, and the standard Polygon BTC/USD aggregator did not match Polymarket Data Streams. The fix was to capture the correct first report at or after open and align clocks with the price-to-beat.

THE BOOK

Best bid and ask were not enough. Quote size, disappearing liquidity, latency and slippage determined whether a theoretical edge could become a real fill. The monitor had to show the difference.

08 / CONTROL REVIEW

The edge disappeared after the patch.

The patch changed the conditions around the strategy. I kept testing the same idea and controlled variants to determine whether the edge was still alive and why execution no longer supported it.

Conclusion: the original edge was dead. The evidence pointed to fee drag, oracle timing, fillability and expanded exposure—not to a new profitable system.
